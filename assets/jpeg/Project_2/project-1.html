Year 01 / Learning the process

Building the foundation

My first season was about learning how the team turned ideas into a working competition robot. I began developing an understanding of manufacturing, machine operation, part inspection, and the relationship between CAD design and the physical assembly.

Learning by making

I worked alongside more experienced students to understand the machining workflow, including how to prepare stock, establish work zero, select tooling, and inspect finished parts. I also began learning how to identify the difference between a design problem, a setup problem, and a machine-accuracy problem.

Focus areas

  • Understanding the CAD-to-CAM workflow
  • Learning safe CNC setup and operation
  • Inspecting parts for fit and dimensional accuracy
  • Connecting manufactured components to robot assembly
Year 02 / Taking ownership

From operator to contributor

As I gained experience, I took on more responsibility for planning and producing robot components. I coordinated with CAD and mechanical teams, organized parts by material and build order, and began helping other students learn the manufacturing workflow.

Keeping production organized

I coordinated manufacturing around the robot's assembly sequence, considering stock size, material, tooling, and which parts were needed first. This helped connect the manufacturing schedule to the mechanical team's progress. I also started explaining not just which steps to follow, but why each setup and inspection step mattered.

Growing responsibilities

  • Organizing part queues and materials
  • Coordinating with CAD and mechanical teams
  • Teaching students CNC workflow fundamentals
  • Documenting repeatable machine procedures
Year 03 / Competition and leadership

Working under real competition pressure

By this stage, I had developed a stronger role in fabrication and troubleshooting. I helped keep production moving during a critical machine failure and worked with the team to diagnose, repair, and recalibrate the CNC router so we could continue producing accurate robot parts.

Solving problems instead of working around them

When the machine could no longer produce accurate parts, we investigated electrical and mechanical causes rather than compensating for the error in CAD or CAM.   • Validated repairs with calibration cuts and dimensional checks

The biggest change over time was moving from completing individual tasks to improving the system around the work. My focus grew to include production planning, student training, machine reliability, documentation, and repeatable quality checks that could help future team members continue the work.

Technical work / 03

Precision Tuning

A machine failure during peak production became a troubleshooting project: identify the source of error, correct the underlying problems, and prove the repair through measurement.

From breakdown to 0.001-inch accuracy

I worked alongside another student during my first year to transition the CNC workflow to Fusion 360 and improve machining accuracy. After the CNC was moved into a new space and rebuilt, I spent a month and a half tuning it to achieve accuracy within 0.001 inch.

During the peak of robotics season, the machine experienced a major failure and could no longer produce accurate parts. My team and I spent 1.5 weeks diagnosing, repairing, and retuning it, ultimately restoring its accuracy to within 0.001 inch. The failure involved operating the machine from a diesel generator while the building was under construction, along with mechanical issues involving track alignment, end stops, and motor mounts.

Project highlights

  • Transitioned the CNC workflow to Fusion 360 Manufacturing
  • Tuned the rebuilt machine to within 0.001 inch
  • Diagnosed and repaired a major failure during peak production
  • Restored machining capability with five days before competition
  • Created tuning, calibration, and troubleshooting documentation
  • Established repeatable validation procedures
01 — Diagnose

Identify the source of error

I compared commanded movement to measured travel, inspected bearing-hole consistency, and separated the problem into electrical, mechanical, and calibration causes instead of compensating in CAD or CAM.

02 — Mechanical calibration

Correct alignment and lost motion

I inspected rack-and-pinion alignment, dual-Y gantry synchronization, motor mounts, end stops, backlash, and steps-per-unit. Mechanical issues were corrected before changing control values.

03 — Electrical investigation

Trace unstable machine behavior

We investigated motor and controller behavior and identified unstable generator power as a major contributor while the building was under construction.

04 — Validation

Prove the repair under production conditions

After each adjustment, I repeated calibration cuts, bearing-hole tests, and dimensional inspections until the machine consistently held the target accuracy.

Problems solved

  • Generator power instability
  • Dual-Y synchronization
  • Rack alignment
  • Motor mount movement
  • End-stop calibration
  • Backlash verification
  • Steps-per-unit tuning
  • Production validation
Result: Helped restore the CNC to 0.001-inch accuracy after a major production failure and returned it to service with five days remaining before competition.
